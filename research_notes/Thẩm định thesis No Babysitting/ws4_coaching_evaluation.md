## A. Problem discovery — how to detect ambiguous objectives, missing context, hidden constraints, unrecognized capabilities, wrong assumptions and unclear acceptance criteria naturally (and do LLMs over- or under-ask?)

### Takeaway
Current LLMs mostly **under-ask** by default: they recognize ambiguity when explicitly asked to judge it, but in normal use they silently commit to one interpretation, and the resulting failures are "silent misframings" that look like clean deliverables. When models *do* ask, a small number of targeted, early, user-answerable questions recovers most of the lost performance, while naive "ask more" prompting flips models into over-asking — so the design problem is a *question policy* (when, what, how many), not a questionnaire.

### Cited Findings

**Default behavior: under-clarification and silent commitment**
- [Finding, BENCH, ACL 2024] CLAMBER (12K examples, taxonomy of ambiguous queries) found "limited practical utility" of off-the-shelf LLMs in identifying and clarifying ambiguous user queries, even with chain-of-thought and few-shot prompting; these techniques produced overconfidence and only marginal gains, and generated clarifying questions were low quality due to "lack of conflict resolution and inaccurate utilization of inherent knowledge." — [CLAMBER, Zhang et al., ACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12063)
- [Finding, BENCH, PRE 2026] "Knowing but Not Showing": models often identify ambiguity when explicitly asked to judge it, yet in QA "overwhelmingly default to direct answers"; adding retrieved context *widened* the gap (more answerable, even less likely to ask). — [Su et al., 2026, arXiv](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:63b8bea2-db5a-4beb-b39b-692e6e6bf7bc/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, BENCH, PRE 2025] ClarifyMT-Bench (6,120 multi-turn dialogues, 5-dimension ambiguity taxonomy, 6 simulated user personas, 10 LLMs): "consistent under-clarification bias: LLMs tend to answer prematurely, and performance degrades as dialogue depth increases." — [Luo et al., 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:c0e592c3-c453-4535-b22d-4897edfafbcd/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, BENCH, ICLR 2026 oral] "LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation": 15 LLMs, 6 generation tasks; when the instruction is revealed in shards over turns (underspecified start), performance drops on average 39% vs. single-turn fully-specified; the drop is mostly unreliability (+112%) rather than aptitude loss (−16%); causes include premature solutions, incorrect assumptions about underspecified details, over-reliance on earlier wrong attempts, verbose replies. When all shards were concatenated into one prompt, performance was 95.1% of baseline. (v1 reported 35%; later versions 39%.) — [Laban et al., arXiv 2505.06120 / ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120)
- [Finding, BENCH, PRE 2026] MT-InfoSeek (5,251 problems, 9,006 instances): models recognize more info is needed but *underestimate how much*; at k=2 in logic they under-predict missing information ~4x as often as over-predict; they often stop before acquiring sufficient information; wrong query order lowers accuracy even when all info is eventually acquired. — [Huang et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:df406689-6c03-4e3d-9252-9a7512a61528/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, BENCH, PRE 2026] Ambig-DS (data-science agents, 112 tasks): failures under ambiguity are "silent commitments" (wrong target / wrong metric) rather than execution errors; allowing *one* clarifying question recovers much of the loss under idealized conditions; but agents can't tell when to use it — "permissive prompts induce over-asking on clear tasks, while conservative prompts induce silent defaulting on ambiguous ones." — [Stoisser et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:fe113dc1-bbee-4514-aa5d-79d56fd94866/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, BENCH, PRE 2026] CIGAsk: "prompting alone is insufficient: models either ask for clarification on every query or ask vague questions that fail to recover the missing information." — [Li et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:595c6d04-82a4-47da-ac57-0ca57dcacae2/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

**Value of asking, and budgets/timing**
- [Finding, BENCH, PRE 2025] Ambig-SWE (underspecified SWE-bench Verified): models "struggle to distinguish between well-specified and underspecified instructions," but when they interact on underspecified inputs, performance improves "up to 74% over the non-interactive settings." — [Vijayvargiya et al., 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:b1386388-790f-4492-bca4-691347f0e8f9/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, BENCH, Findings of EMNLP 2026] "Ask or Assume?": an uncertainty-aware scaffold that *decouples underspecification detection from execution* reached 69.40% resolve rate on underspecified SWE-bench Verified, closing the gap to fully specified instructions, and showed calibrated behavior — "conserving queries on simple tasks while proactively seeking information on more complex issues." — [Edwards et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:4fff7875-1a05-4c44-93e3-d570647cd685/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, BENCH, Findings of ACL 2026] SAGE-Agent: Expected Value of Perfect Information (EVPI) per candidate question + cost model; 7–39% higher coverage on ambiguous tasks while asking 1.5–2.7x *fewer* clarification questions than prompting/uncertainty baselines; separates *specification uncertainty* (what the user wants) from *model uncertainty*. — [Suri et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:3719af63-e115-43b7-b94b-68b9b915621a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, BENCH, PRE 2026] CLARITI (8B clarification module): effective questions have two properties — *task relevance* (information that predicts success) and *user answerability* (what users can realistically provide); matched GPT-5's resolution rate on underspecified issues with 41% fewer questions. — [Vijayvargiya et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:30c8eee7-dc04-46d6-97bb-42a2f625bed6/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, BENCH, PRE 2026] "Ask Early, Ask Late, Ask Right" (6,000+ runs, 4 frontier models, 4 info dimensions: goal, input, constraint, context): *goal* clarification loses nearly all value after ~10% of execution (pass@3 0.78 → baseline); *input* clarification retains value until ~50%; deferring any clarification past mid-trajectory is worse than never asking. In 300 unscripted sessions, no frontier model asked within the optimal window; behaviors ranged from over-asking (52% of sessions) to never asking. — [Gulati et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:80ea5c02-e9a1-4378-b223-f088f1a8897b/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, BENCH, PRE 2026] Information-gain-trained clarifier on τ-Bench: +3.7% success over no-clarification while adding only ~0.3 interaction steps on average. — [Deng et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:bb17122f-39ba-4afd-aa86-16a6cc871756/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, BENCH, PRE 2025] Active Task Disambiguation frames clarification as Bayesian experimental design: choosing questions that maximize information gain over the *space of viable solutions* beats reasoning only in the space of questions. — [Kobalczyk et al., 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:21a88dca-ecbf-484b-a99f-0e18c32e90e5/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Fact, PRE 2026] LHAW provides a taxonomy for removing information along four dimensions — Goals, Constraints, Inputs, Context — and classifies variants as outcome-critical, divergent, or benign by observed outcome divergence (not by LLM guesses of ambiguity). — [Pu et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:3d912f6c-871f-491f-b8a7-fa0b73d3bbcf/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, PRE 2024] Training with preference labels based on simulated *future turns* teaches models when to ask vs. answer directly (+5% F1 on multi-interpretation answer sets; +3% accuracy on ask/answer judgments). — [Zhang et al., 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:8c40edf7-3225-4919-830d-4fc655502eeb/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

**Human requirements-elicitation evidence**
- [Finding, systematic review, IEEE RE 2006] Davis et al.: structured interviews appear among the most effective elicitation techniques; card sorting, ranking and think-aloud tend to underperform interviews; analyst experience did not seem to matter much; intermediate representations showed no clear benefit; authors caution that underlying studies were not replicated. — [Davis, Dieste, Hickey, Juristo, Moreno, RE 2006](https://ebse.webspace.durham.ac.uk/ebse-bibliography/effectiveness-of-requirements-elicitation-techniques-empirical-results-derived-from-a-systematic-review/)

**Problem framing as a distinct failure mode in human-AI work**
- [Finding, FE, HBS WP 2023] BCG "jagged frontier" (preregistered, 758 consultants ≈7% of individual-contributor consultants): on a task *outside* the AI frontier, consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to produce correct solutions than those without AI; inside the frontier, +12.2% tasks completed, 25.1% faster, and (per working-paper abstract) >40% higher quality. — [Dell'Acqua et al., HBS WP 24-013 / SSRN 4573321](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Inference] The six NB discovery targets map cleanly onto the four dimensions used in the clarification literature (Goal / Constraint / Input / Context — LHAW; Gulati et al.): *ambiguous objectives* and *unclear acceptance criteria* = Goal; *hidden constraints* = Constraint; *missing context* = Input/Context; *wrong assumptions* = a model's silent commitment (Ambig-DS); *unrecognized AI capabilities* is the one dimension **not** covered by this literature — it is a user-side knowledge gap (the user doesn't know what to ask for), closer to the BCG "frontier" problem than to ambiguity resolution.
- [Inference] Because base models under-ask and prompt tweaks swing them to over-asking (Ambig-DS; CIGAsk), a defensible NB contribution is a **separate discovery step decoupled from execution** — the architecture that worked best in "Ask or Assume?" — rather than hoping the execution platform asks well. This also fits B3 (NB owns discovery; execution delegated).
- [Inference] The evidence supports a *question budget* shaped by (a) expected value of the answer, (b) whether the user can answer it, (c) timing. Goal and acceptance-criteria questions must come before work starts (value collapses after ~10% of execution); input/context questions can be deferred to when they are needed.
- [Recommendation] "Natural, not a questionnaire" discovery pattern for NB, grounded in the above:
  1. **Restate-and-assume first**: draft a short problem frame (goal, deliverable, acceptance check, constraints, assumptions) and show the assumptions explicitly, so the user corrects rather than answers from scratch. (Grounded in: silent-commitment failures are the main risk; correcting a visible assumption is cheaper than an open question — [Inference] not directly tested.)
  2. **Ask only outcome-critical, user-answerable questions**, ranked by expected impact; typical budget 1–3 up-front questions (consistent with Ambig-DS "one question recovers much," SAGE/CLARITI fewer-questions results). No invented hard cap — the budget should be calibrated in NB's own experiment.
  3. **Goal & acceptance criteria first, inputs later** (Gulati et al. timing curves).
  4. **Capability surfacing as a proposal, not a question**: "This could also be done by X (e.g., running the tests / reading the repo / a scheduled task) — want that?" since users can't ask about capabilities they don't know exist ([Inference] from BCG frontier and AI Fluency "Delegation" competency).
  5. **Prefer structured-interview logic hidden behind conversation** (Davis et al.: structured interviews outperform other elicitation techniques), i.e., a checklist the system tracks internally, not a form shown to the user.
- [Hypothesis] A "visible-assumption frame + 1–3 high-EVPI questions" step will improve outcome quality on ambiguous real tasks relative to plain chat use, with less friction than open-ended Socratic questioning. Untested with real knowledge workers; all strong evidence above is from benchmarks with simulated users.

### Gaps
- Nearly all clarification evidence is BENCH with simulated users (coding, QA, tool use). I found no RCT with real knowledge workers measuring how many clarifying questions they tolerate before abandoning, or how question budgets affect satisfaction in open-ended professional tasks.
- No direct evidence found on detecting "unrecognized AI capabilities" (capability blindness) as a discovery target; the Socratic-tutoring literature for problem *framing* (vs. tutoring known content) was not covered in this session.
- Exact effect sizes for elicitation techniques (Davis et al.) were not retrieved; only qualitative conclusions.
- The 40% quality figure in Dell'Acqua et al. is from the working-paper abstract and was not re-verified in the published version.

## B. Adaptive coaching — what to adapt, what to infer vs. ask vs. confirm, and whether users want coaching or just results

### Takeaway
Learning science gives a clear rule — guidance helps novices and can hurt experts (expertise reversal), so guidance must be faded as competence grows — and the AI-specific evidence shows that unguided AI use raises short-term performance while lowering later unaided performance, comprehension and critical engagement. But the interventions that reduce over-reliance most are the ones users like least, and most real usage is "just give me the result," so coaching must be lightweight, opt-in-adjustable and tied to stakes, not constant.

### Cited Findings

**What adaptive tutoring achieves**
- [Finding, meta-review, Educational Psychologist 2011] VanLehn: human tutoring d ≈ 0.79 vs. no tutoring; step-based ITS d ≈ 0.76 (28 effects); substep-based ≈ 0.40; an "interaction plateau" where human, substep and step tutoring perform about equally; did not confirm Bloom's 2-sigma. — [VanLehn 2011, ERIC EJ946764](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ946764); [VanLehn slides](https://quality.mit.edu/files/2012/01/Quality-Symposium-Kurt-VanLehn.pdf)
- [Finding, RCT, Scientific Reports 2025] Harvard physics (N=194): a *structured, pedagogically-designed* AI tutor produced larger learning gains than in-class active learning; linear regression effect 0.63 SD (underestimated due to ceiling), quantile-regression estimates reported as ~0.73–1.3 SD (secondary). No delayed test; unexplained missing post-test scores (142 AI vs. 174 classroom, per a secondary review). — [Kestin et al., Sci Rep 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/); [ETC Journal review](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/)
- [Finding, meta-analysis, JCAL 2022] Metacognitive prompts in computer-based learning: g = 0.50 on SRL activities and g = 0.40 on learning outcomes; effects moderated by feedback, specificity and *adaptability* of prompts. — [Guo 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:539f3264-cc4f-467e-97c9-2b8a92524cf2/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

**Expertise reversal → fade guidance**
- [Fact/Finding, review, Educ Psych Review 2007] Expertise reversal effect: instructional techniques effective for novices lose effectiveness and can harm more experienced learners, attributed to cognitive load from processing redundant guidance. — [Kalyuga 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs10648-007-9054-3)
- [Finding, meta-analysis, Learning and Instruction 2025] Tetzlaff et al.: (secondary summary) 176 effect sizes, 60 experiments, 5,924 participants; effect robust; moderated by educational status and domain; *helping novices matters more than withholding help from experts*. Pooled effect size not verified. — [secondary summary via search results](https://theelearningcoach.com/?p=7716) (primary not retrieved — unverified)
- [Finding, FE, QJE 2025] Customer-support agents (5,172 agents): AI assistance raised resolutions/hour ~14% on average (WP; QJE release says 15%), ~34% for novice/low-skill workers (WP figure); the most experienced and skilled saw small speed gains and *small declines in quality*. — [Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond, QJE 2025](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:140:y:2025:i:2:p:889-942.)

**Over-reliance, deskilling, cognitive offloading**
- [Finding, FE, PNAS 2025] Bastani et al. (~1,000 high-school math students, Turkey): while available, GPT Base improved practice grades by 48% and a safeguarded "GPT Tutor" by 127%; when access was removed, GPT Base students did worse than never-access controls (17% reduction per the SSRN preprint; not re-verified in PNAS version), while GPT Tutor largely mitigated the harm (scored about the same as control). Mechanism: most GPT Base students asked for the answer. — [Bastani et al., PNAS 2025 (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html); [Wharton summary](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/); [Stanford SCALE summary](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/generative-ai-can-harm-learning)
- [Finding, RCT, Anthropic/arXiv 2026] Shen & Tamkin (52 Python developers learning the unfamiliar Trio library): the AI-assisted group scored lower on an immediate comprehension quiz (≈17% lower per secondary summaries), largest gap on debugging; the speed gain was small and not statistically significant; participants who used AI to build comprehension (follow-up/conceptual questions) did better; full delegation sacrificed learning. Short horizon, single library. — [Anthropic research post, Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills); [arXiv 2601.20245](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20245)
- [Finding, SURV, CHI 2025] Lee et al. (Microsoft Research/CMU), n=319 knowledge workers (reported 936 first-hand examples — secondary): higher confidence in GenAI associated with *less* critical thinking; higher self-confidence associated with *more* critical thinking (perceived as more effort); critical thinking shifts from production to verification, response integration and "task stewardship"; inhibitors include lack of awareness of AI limitations and difficulty improving AI output. Self-report, correlational. — [Lee et al., CHI 2025 PDF](https://advait.org/files/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf)
- [Finding, LAB, PRE 2025] "Your Brain on ChatGPT" (MIT Media Lab; 54 participants, 18 in crossover session 4; essay writing; EEG): LLM group showed weakest neural connectivity, lowest essay ownership, trouble quoting own essays; authors frame as "cognitive debt." Not peer-reviewed; small sample; single model; does not show brain damage. — [Kosmyna et al., MIT Media Lab 2025](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/your-brain-on-chatgpt/)
- [Finding, LAB, Computers in Human Behavior 2025] Fernandes et al. (LSAT logical reasoning): AI raised task scores but users *overestimated* their performance; higher AI literacy → *more* confident but *less* accurate self-assessment; the Dunning-Kruger pattern disappeared; monetary incentive did not fix overestimation; human+AI did not exceed AI alone; most users used single prompts without reflection. — [Fernandes et al., Aalto](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/ai-makes-you-smarter-but-none-the-wiser-the-disconnect-between-pe/); [arXiv 2409.16708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16708v1)
- [Finding, systematic review, AI & Society 2025] Automation bias review (35 studies, 2015–2025): explanations often increase acceptability but are insufficient to reduce automation bias; factors include AI literacy, expertise, verification demands; "user engagement emerges as the most feasible and impactful point of intervention." — [Romeo et al., 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:94f2b33c-1128-4177-bf58-12774ee7564d/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

**Interventions against over-reliance and the friction trade-off**
- [Finding, LAB, CSCW/PACM-HCI 2021] Buçinca et al. (N=199): cognitive forcing functions (e.g., decide before seeing AI) significantly reduced over-reliance vs. simple XAI, **but participants gave the least favorable ratings to the designs that reduced over-reliance most**; benefits concentrated in people high in Need for Cognition (intervention-generated inequality). — [Buçinca et al., 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:73713e06-5efc-4c9b-b0da-7408bdb871aa/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LAB, PACM-HCI 2023] Vasconcelos et al. (5 studies, N=731): over-reliance is a *strategic cost-benefit choice*; explanations reduce over-reliance when they lower the cost of verification; task difficulty and incentives shift reliance. — [Vasconcelos et al., 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:9e953f16-e06d-44dc-96a9-21da7b450b97/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LAB, IUI 2026] Trust-adaptive interventions (supporting explanations when trust is low, counter-explanations when trust is high, adaptive forced pauses): up to 38% reduction in inappropriate reliance and 20% improvement in decision accuracy, incl. medical doctors. — [Srinivasan et al., IUI 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:316dbca9-d320-4c9d-bc8c-e8fe592db977/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LAB, PRE 2026] Cognitive forcing on AI-generated *execution plans* in writing: an "Assumption" CFF (argument/assumption analysis) most reduced over-reliance *without increasing cognitive load*; participants perceived a "WhatIf" CFF as most helpful (perceived ≠ effective). — [Ghosh et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:30026832-d716-4f1e-aea9-92fee8fa6b3d/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LAB, CHI 2026] Guided reflection (N=178) reduced over-reliance and improved accuracy vs. AI and XAI; again benefited high Need-for-Cognition users more. — [Li et al., CHI 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:7d694d6e-189b-4085-ac83-602b40e58d48/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LAB, 2025/2026] Diabetes meal-planning (N=108): confidence levels, text explanations and performance visualization improved accuracy; human-feedback and AI-generated questions prompted reflection but *reduced performance* and trust due to added effort. — [Henderson 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:bbe40664-b069-4093-97ed-684e679eaca9/?utm_source=claude_desktop); [Chen et al., 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:924cfa9f-ce0b-4abf-b313-ea38aa27a9d3/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LAB, PACM-HCI 2024] Always-on second opinions reduced over-reliance but increased under-reliance; *user-solicited* second opinions mitigated over-reliance without increasing under-reliance in some cases. — [Lu et al., 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:bebacfc4-97db-44d1-84c1-943f3f441df4/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LAB, PACM-HCI 2025] Partial explanations reduced over-reliance relative to none (N=264, N=210), less than full explanations. — [de Jong et al., 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:718a64b6-69b6-415b-970a-a890557d488e/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

**Do users want coaching or results? (friction tolerance)**
- [Finding, LOG, Anthropic Apr 2025] Education Report (574,740 student conversations after filtering, from ~1M): ~47% were "direct," minimal-effort conversations seeking quick resolution; Creating (39.8%) and Analyzing (30.2%) were the most common Bloom levels delegated (Bloom percentages from secondary coverage). — [Anthropic Education Report](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-education-report-how-university-students-use-claude); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/students-delegate-higher-level-thinking-to-ai-anthropic-study-finds/)
- [Finding, LOG, Anthropic Feb 2026] AI Fluency Index (9,830 Claude.ai multi-turn conversations, one week Jan 2026; 11 of 24 4D behavioral indicators observable): iteration/refinement is the strongest correlate of other fluency behaviors; augmentative "thought-partner" conversations showed more than double the fluency behaviors of quick exchanges; **when artifacts were produced, all three observable Discernment behaviors decreased** (users less likely to check polished outputs). — [Anthropic, AI Fluency Index](https://anthropic.com/research/AI-fluency-index); [Anthropic Academy](https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/the-ai-fluency-index)
- [Fact] Platform "learning modes" exist (ChatGPT Study Mode, Claude Learning Mode, Gemini Guided Learning), but I found no peer-reviewed evaluation of their learning outcomes as of Nov 2026; OpenAI stated it is working with Stanford SCALE on outcome research. — [Outlook Business on Study Mode](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/artificial-intelligence/openai-launches-study-mode-in-chatgpt-to-promote-active-learning-check-ai-dependency); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91439393/ai-tutor-chatgpt-openai)
- [Fact, report] A May 2026 report (based on spotted code references, not a confirmed launch) says Anthropic may turn the AI Fluency research into a personal "fluency scorecard" in Claude settings. — [TestingCatalog](https://testingcatalog.com/anthropic-to-introduce-personal-ai-fluency-scorecard-in-claude) (unverified)

### Inferences
- [Inference] The adaptation dimensions in the brief (guidance intensity, question depth, challenge, autonomy, workflow complexity, explanation, verification) collapse into two evidence-backed levers: **(1) how much the AI does vs. the human (autonomy/guidance, faded by competence — expertise reversal; Brynjolfsson quality decline for experts)** and **(2) how much verification/engagement is required (scaled by stakes and by the AI's distance from its frontier — BCG, Lee et al., Fluency Index discernment drop)**. Explanation depth and question depth are sub-settings of these.
- [Inference] The Bastani GPT-Tutor vs. GPT-Base contrast and the Anthropic Trio study both show that *how* AI is used (answer-seeking vs. comprehension-seeking) determines learning, not AI access per se. This supports NB's premise that collaboration *style* matters — but these are learning contexts, not professional outcome contexts.
- [Inference] Friction evidence cuts against heavy coaching: the most effective anti-over-reliance designs are disliked (Buçinca), benefit mainly high-NFC users, and some reflection prompts reduce task performance (Henderson/Chen). Roughly half of observed usage is direct/minimal-effort (Anthropic Education Report). This supports counter-thesis (b) "users want results" for the *median* interaction, while leaving room for coaching on high-stakes or out-of-frontier tasks.
- [Inference] Perceived helpfulness diverges from effectiveness (Ghosh: WhatIf perceived most helpful; Assumption most effective), so NB cannot tune coaching by satisfaction ratings alone.
- [Recommendation] Infer / Ask / Confirm policy (consistent with B5 risk-based adaptation):
  | Decision | Default | Evidence basis |
  |---|---|---|
  | Task stakes, reversibility, whether a verification check exists | **Infer** from task, then show as part of the frame | Verification demand drives automation bias (Romeo 2025) |
  | User's domain expertise for *this* task | **Infer weakly** from how they describe it; **ask** only if it changes the plan | Expertise reversal; self-assessment unreliable (Fernandes) |
  | Goal, deliverable, acceptance criteria | **Ask/confirm** up front (visible assumptions) | Clarification timing (Gulati) |
  | Guidance level for this session | **Propose** a default; one-tap more/less | Lu 2024 (user-solicited support works better than always-on) |
  | Persistent preference / autonomy level / "user profile" | **Confirm** explicitly; user-editable | OLM literature (see D); wrong-profiling risk |
  | Verification step before accepting output | **Auto-insert** a lightweight check when stakes are high or output is polished | Fluency Index discernment drop; Assumption-CFF low load |
- [Recommendation] Prefer low-load engagement moves (surface assumptions in the AI's plan, ask "what would make this wrong?", self-generated acceptance check *before* seeing output) over lectures or quizzes; make coaching *solicitable* rather than always-on.
- [Hypothesis] For professional (non-learning) tasks, a "stakes-gated" coaching policy (coach only when stakes high, task likely outside AI frontier, or user asks) preserves most benefit with acceptable friction. Untested.

### Gaps
- No evidence found on coaching efficacy for *adult professionals doing real work* (most evidence is students, lab tasks, or call-center agents). The workplace evidence (BCG, Brynjolfsson, METR) measures AI access, not coaching.
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of ChatGPT Study Mode / Claude Learning Mode outcomes found.
- Desirable difficulties (Bjork) and scaffolding/fading primary sources (Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976) were not retrieved this session; they are well-established background but uncited here.
- Willingness-to-pay or stated demand for "being coached" vs. "getting outputs" among knowledge workers not found (WS6 territory).
- Exact Tetzlaff 2025 pooled effect size unverified; Bloom-level percentages from Anthropic Education Report are from secondary coverage.

## C. Evaluation on four dimensions — indicators, evidence, methods, limitations, human review, Goodhart risk, cost; reliability of LLM-as-judge and self-report; existing frameworks

### Takeaway
Task outcome can be evaluated reasonably when an external check exists (tests, acceptance criteria, rubric + human spot-check), but LLM judges carry measurable position, style/verbosity, self-preference and leniency biases, and their exact-match agreement overstates validity. Self-report is the weakest signal: in the METR RCT developers believed AI made them ~20% faster while it made them 19% slower, and AI users systematically overestimate their own performance — so collaboration and capability dimensions must rely on behavioral evidence and delayed/unaided checks, not ratings.

### Cited Findings

**LLM-as-judge reliability**
- [Finding, NeurIPS 2023] GPT-4 as judge reached >80% agreement with human preferences on MT-Bench/Chatbot Arena, similar to human–human agreement; documented position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases and limited reasoning on math. — [Zheng et al., 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:56a92d17-c930-409a-bfbc-5fb06226957e/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, PRE 2026] Largest audit to date (21 judges, 9 providers, ~541,000 judgments): exact-match agreement overstates discrimination — kappa deflation of 33–41 pp on MT-Bench; judge rankings shift up to 14 positions across benchmarks; high test-retest reliability (>0.95) coexists with severe position bias (>0.10) in two production judges ("consistency–bias paradox"); verbosity bias small (<0.011) under a single pairwise rubric. Proposes a Minimum Viable Validation Protocol. — [Norman et al., 2026, "Reliability without Validity"](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:5e4436ac-1785-4ee0-8bf4-c52c04759020/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, PRE 2026] 9 debiasing strategies × 5 judges: best agreement 71.0% (κ=0.549) for Gemini 2.5 Flash with debiasing vs. 69.5% for Claude Sonnet 4; **style bias dominant (0.10–0.76, favoring markdown over plain prose)**, far exceeding position bias (≤0.04); verbosity bias heterogeneous by model (some prefer longer, Claude prefers concise). — [Soumik, 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:a62fc5fd-1356-441f-acd7-c2964ac456d2/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, PRE 2024] 13 judges in a clean high-human-agreement setting: only the largest models reach reasonable alignment and still lag inter-human agreement; scores may differ by up to 5 points from humans; leniency tendency; percent agreement can hide large score differences. — [Thakur et al., 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:b470b80d-53e5-4bd3-aec1-c71ad949cc00/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, NeurIPS 2024] LLMs recognize their own generations and self-recognition correlates linearly with self-preference bias. — [Panickssery et al., 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:e8c83980-3fb7-45fd-b39c-742792be31ec/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, PRE 2024] Self-preference linked to lower perplexity (familiarity) of text, regardless of authorship. — [Wataoka et al., 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:97e577f6-efa8-4e98-a85a-18f5fc5a7fd5/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, PRE 2025] Statistical self-bias estimation (>5,000 prompt-completion pairs with expert human annotations, 9 judges): GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet systematically score their own outputs higher, and show *family bias*. — [Spiliopoulou et al., 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:2d619eab-7fc8-47cb-88ab-ca7f81a6193f/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, Findings of EMNLP 2026] Rubric-based judging behaves like multiple choice: model-specific position bias over score options, and criterion ordering shifts scores; a few random permutations reduce the error. — [Xu et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:1c1828b5-0e58-470f-935f-1510727e2e13/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, PRE medRxiv 2026] 71 human experts vs. 6 LLM judges on clinical free text: strong AI self-preference; AI scores correlated with length and lexical diversity (human scores didn't); long responses kept high scores even when shuffled to the wrong question. — [Alvarez-Arenas et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:56d259d9-2cb9-47b3-b208-c15643a4d597/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, PRE 2026] On LLMBar (adversarial: superficially better answer is worse), 50 judges' verdicts agreed with humans only 0.456 even after order averaging. — [Mukherjee et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:10cbec4a-5bc8-4c9a-a84b-21d44f30063b/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, PRE 2026] Judge upgrades are not interchangeable; stronger judges reduce but do not remove position/verbosity bias; repeated-sample juries add little when errors are correlated. — [Yang et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:c1446279-afd3-40f3-9ce2-dc468afd25fb/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, PRE 2024] CALM framework identifies 12 bias types; significant biases persist in specific tasks. — [Ye et al., 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:156b5403-981a-493c-b0d9-48960aba54ac/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, EMNLP 2024] Both human and LLM judges are vulnerable to misinformation-oversight, authority and "beauty" (presentation) biases. — [Chen et al., EMNLP 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:c985c079-3320-44a0-970d-1898345852d0/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, ICLR 2024] Sycophancy: in hh-rlhf preference data, responses matching user views are more likely preferred; humans and preference models prefer convincingly-written sycophantic responses over correct ones a non-negligible fraction of the time; optimizing against PMs sometimes trades truthfulness for sycophancy. — [Sharma et al., ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0105f7972202c1d4fb817da9f21a9663-Abstract-Conference.html)

**Self-report and perceived productivity**
- [Finding, RCT, METR Jul 2025] 16 experienced OSS developers, 246 real tasks on mature repos (Cursor + Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet): AI allowed → completion time **+19%**; developers forecast −24% and, after the study, still estimated AI had sped them up ~20%. Authors caution about generalization and future models. — [METR, arXiv 2507.09089](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089); [Simon Willison summary](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jul/12/ai-open-source-productivity/)
- [Finding, LAB, CHB 2025] AI users overestimate performance; incentives don't fix it; higher AI literacy → worse calibration. — [Fernandes et al.](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/ai-makes-you-smarter-but-none-the-wiser-the-disconnect-between-pe/)
- [Finding, LAB] Perceived-most-helpful CFF ≠ most effective CFF. — [Ghosh et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:30026832-d716-4f1e-aea9-92fee8fa6b3d/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LAB] Designs that most reduce over-reliance get the lowest subjective ratings. — [Buçinca et al., 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:73713e06-5efc-4c9b-b0da-7408bdb871aa/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LOG] Polished artifacts reduce users' discernment behaviors. — [Anthropic AI Fluency Index, 2026](https://anthropic.com/research/AI-fluency-index)

**Existing frameworks for human-AI collaboration / fluency**
- [Fact] Anthropic 4D AI Fluency framework (with Rick Dakan & Joseph Feller): Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence; 24 behavioral indicators, of which 11 are observable in chat; the unobservable ones are "arguably some of the most consequential." — [Anthropic AI Fluency Index](https://anthropic.com/research/AI-fluency-index)
- [Fact, CHI 2019] Microsoft HAX: 18 guidelines in 4 phases (initially, during interaction, when wrong, over time), distilled from 150+ recommendations, evaluated with 49 design practitioners against 20 AI products; developed on GUI products. — [Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)
- [Fact, CHI 2025] Lee et al. operationalize critical thinking in GenAI use via Bloom-based activities and observe shift to verification, integration and stewardship — a usable indicator set for collaboration quality. — [Lee et al., CHI 2025](https://advait.org/files/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf)
- [Fact] Clarification benchmarks (Ambig-SWE, LHAW, Ambig-DS) score *detect → ask → use* as separate steps, a usable template for evaluating problem framing. — [Ambig-SWE](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:b1386388-790f-4492-bca4-691347f0e8f9/?utm_source=claude_desktop); [Ambig-DS](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:fe113dc1-bbee-4514-aa5d-79d56fd94866/?utm_source=claude_desktop)

### Inferences
- [Inference] A single LLM judge, from the same family as the generating model, scoring polished markdown output, is the worst case on every documented bias axis (self/family preference, style, verbosity, leniency). NB evaluation of *outcomes produced on Claude/ChatGPT* using a Claude/GPT judge will inherit self/family bias.
- [Inference] Because self-report diverges from measured outcomes in exactly NB's domain (perceived productivity, perceived skill, perceived helpfulness), any NB validation that relies on "users say it helped" is structurally weak. Voluntary reuse and verified outcome checks are better primary signals (aligns with WS6 metrics).
- [Recommendation] Four-dimension evaluation framework (no numeric scales invented; use checklists + pairwise + human spot-check):

  | Dimension | Indicators | Observable evidence | Methods | Main limitations | Human review needed when | Goodhart risk | Cost |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | (1) Task Outcome Quality | Meets user-confirmed acceptance criteria; correctness; fitness for purpose; rework needed | Tests pass, criteria checklist, artifact diff, downstream acceptance (merged, sent, used) | Executable checks first; rubric derived from the *user's own* acceptance criteria; pairwise vs. baseline (blind, order-swapped, cross-family judge); user confirmation | Many knowledge tasks lack ground truth; judge biases; late-arriving real outcomes | No executable check exists; high stakes; judge confidence low or judges disagree | Optimizing to the rubric/criteria rather than the real need; "looks polished" | Low with tests; medium with rubric+judge; high with experts |
  | (2) Problem Framing Quality | Goal/deliverable/acceptance criteria explicit; constraints & assumptions surfaced; outcome-critical questions asked early; reframes accepted | Frame artifact before execution; number of later goal changes/rework caused by misframing; whether surfaced assumptions were corrected | Checklist on the frame (Goal/Constraint/Input/Context — LHAW); retrospective: "did the final deliverable require info that was knowable but not elicited?"; counterfactual: framing vs. no framing on same task | Hard to know the "right" frame; hindsight bias; users may accept a frame passively (sycophancy in reverse) | Disagreement over what the problem was; strategic tasks | Producing long frames/many questions to look thorough (over-asking) | Low–medium |
  | (3) Human-AI Collaboration Quality | Appropriate reliance (accepts correct, rejects/corrects wrong); iteration; verification before accepting; delegation of suitable sub-tasks; use of relevant native capabilities | Edits/corrections to AI output; verification actions; follow-up questions; whether user caught seeded or real errors; Fluency-type behaviors | Behavioral traces (4D observable indicators); occasional seeded-error checks (with consent); process rubric scored by judge + human calibration | Many behaviors happen off-platform (unobservable); behaviors ≠ quality; privacy | Interpreting off-platform behavior; when profiling users | Users perform "checking theater"; counting iterations rewards inefficiency | Medium |
  | (4) User Capability Development | Unaided or less-aided performance on a *later, comparable* task; better frames produced without prompting; calibrated self-assessment | Transfer task performance; fading of needed guidance; self-assessment vs. measured accuracy | Delayed/transfer tasks; within-user comparisons across tasks; occasional "no-AI" or "less-help" probes (opt-in) | Needs time and multiple sessions (T2 tension); task difficulty confounds; learning vs. novelty effects | Always, for any claim of durable skill | Teaching to the probe; users gaming self-reports | High (longitudinal) |

- [Recommendation] LLM-judge hygiene for NB (all derived from the cited bias studies): use the user's acceptance criteria as the rubric; judge pairwise with order swapped; use a judge from a different model family than the generator; strip/normalize formatting before judging (style bias); permute rubric option order; report kappa not raw agreement; validate against a small human-labeled set before trusting; route low-confidence or split decisions to human review.
- [Recommendation] Dimension 4 should be *out of scope for an MVP claim* (it cannot be proven without multi-session data — tension T2); dimensions 1–2 can be tested within a single session against a baseline; dimension 3 partially.

### Gaps
- No validated instrument found that measures "human-AI collaboration quality" for open-ended professional work end-to-end; the 4D Index measures 11 chat-observable behaviors correlationally and does not link them to outcome quality.
- No study found linking better problem framing to better task outcomes for real knowledge workers (only benchmark analogs with simulated users).
- No evidence found on LLM-judge reliability for evaluating *problem frames* or *collaboration process* (judge research is about answer quality).
- METR's later follow-ups (if any, 2026) were not checked in this session; the 2025 result applies to early-2025 tools and experienced developers on familiar repos.
- Costs (dollar/time) of human review for NB-type tasks: no data; only one judge-cost datum (≈$0.001 vs. ≈$0.015 per evaluation in Soumik 2026).

## D. The learning loop (Evaluation → Adaptation → Better Collaboration → Better Outcomes) — evidence it works, uncertainty, wrong profiling, user inspection/correction, task performance vs. durable capability

### Takeaway
Closed adaptive loops work in well-structured tutoring domains (ITS d ≈ 0.76; adaptive metacognitive prompts g ≈ 0.40), and open learner models show that letting users inspect and negotiate the system's model of them supports reflection and model accuracy — but there is no evidence yet that such loops work for open-ended professional AI collaboration, and the AI evidence repeatedly shows that short-term performance with AI is a misleading proxy for durable capability. The loop must therefore treat its observations as uncertain hypotheses, show them to the user, and measure capability only through delayed or less-aided transfer.

### Cited Findings
- [Finding, meta-review] Step-based ITS (which model the learner and adapt per step) achieve d ≈ 0.76 vs. no tutoring, near human tutoring (0.79). — [VanLehn 2011](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ946764)
- [Finding, meta-analysis] Adaptive, specific, feedback-bearing metacognitive prompts produce larger effects; overall g = 0.40 on learning outcomes. — [Guo 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:539f3264-cc4f-467e-97c9-2b8a92524cf2/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Fact/Finding, IEEE TLT 2020] Open Learner Models (OLMs): opening the learner model prompts reflection and planning; learner–system discussion/editing of the model lets users "contribute more directly to the modeling process to increase its accuracy" while further facilitating reflection. — [Bull, 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:a6440a05-6673-48a8-991b-1a85d92207a8/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, systematic review, Computers & Education 2020] 64 OLM studies in higher education: OLMs mainly support cognition, less metacognition/motivation; although editable/negotiable models exist, **a simple inspectable OLM is more preferred**; models relying on unobservable nodes are disfavored because they need costly expert authoring. — [Hooshyar et al., 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:c06cc634-68b8-4709-9917-e169646f6f67/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, meta-synthesis, Frontiers in Education 2026] 26 studies: continuum Inspectable → Negotiable → Editable → Persuasive/Adaptive; inspectable models support monitoring/awareness; negotiable models foster calibration and dialogic feedback; editable models strengthen autonomy and goal-setting. — [Robles Mucho et al., 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:cd6ce20f-e25e-419f-8423-e629786c9522/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LAK 2018] OLM review: ~60% of OLMs use a single data type; 39% support user input; OLM research emphasizes learner control and access to own data. — [Bodily et al., 2018](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:1e731828-898a-4434-abb9-97a1624aec1a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Fact, IJAIED 2020] Winne argues learner models should include *process* trace data (how the learner works), not only achievement, to inform self-regulation — closest analog to modeling "collaboration process." — [Winne, 2020](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:e1127e9a-1243-4237-b699-ed7999acfb0a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, FE] Performance-with-AI vs. performance-after-AI diverge: Bastani GPT Base +48% in practice but worse than control when access removed; GPT Tutor +127% in practice with roughly no harm on exam. — [Bastani et al., PNAS 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html); [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/)
- [Finding, RCT] Immediate comprehension lower with AI assistance in a new-skill setting; style of use moderated it. — [Shen & Tamkin, Anthropic 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)
- [Finding, FE] Call-center AI exposure accelerated learning curves (secondary summary: 2 months with AI ≈ 6+ months without) — evidence that AI-embedded guidance can transfer into worker skill in a narrow, repetitive domain. — [Brynjolfsson et al., QJE 2025 (summary)](https://causalinf.substack.com/p/economics-of-generative-ai-on-workers) (secondary; verify in QJE text)
- [Finding, LAB] Adaptive (trust-state-dependent) interventions outperform static ones for reliance calibration (up to 38% less inappropriate reliance). — [Srinivasan et al., IUI 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:316dbca9-d320-4c9d-bc8c-e8fe592db977/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding, LAB] Interventions benefit high Need-for-Cognition users more → adaptation can widen gaps. — [Buçinca et al., 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:73713e06-5efc-4c9b-b0da-7408bdb871aa/?utm_source=claude_desktop); [Li et al., CHI 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:7d694d6e-189b-4085-ac83-602b40e58d48/?utm_source=claude_desktop)
- [Finding] Self-assessment is miscalibrated under AI use, so user self-reports are a noisy input to the loop. — [Fernandes et al., 2025](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/ai-makes-you-smarter-but-none-the-wiser-the-disconnect-between-pe/); [METR 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089)
- [Fact, CHI 2019] HAX guidelines include over-time behaviors such as learning from user behavior, updating and adapting cautiously, encouraging granular feedback, conveying consequences of user actions, and providing global controls (guideline names per the published set). — [Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)

### Inferences
- [Inference] The proven loops (ITS, OLM) rely on a *structured domain model* (known skills, correct answers). NB's domain — framing unclear problems and collaborating with AI — has no such ground-truth skill lattice, so the transferable part is the *pattern* (evidence → tentative model → visible to user → negotiated → adapt), not the precise modeling.
- [Inference] Wrong-profiling risk is high because each session is a small, noisy, task-confounded sample: a user who skips verification may be (a) over-reliant, (b) expert on that task, or (c) on a low-stakes task. Expertise reversal means mis-classifying an expert as a novice actively harms them; mis-classifying a novice as expert removes needed help (and Tetzlaff 2025, secondary, says the latter matters more).
- [Inference] Reinforcing bad habits is a real loop failure: if adaptation optimizes satisfaction or speed (which users report inaccurately), it will drift toward less verification and more delegation — the exact pattern linked to lower discernment and later unaided performance.
- [Recommendation] Learning-loop design principles:
  1. **Observations as hypotheses with evidence**: store "I noticed X in tasks A, B (e.g., accepted outputs without checking on 2 high-stakes tasks)" rather than trait labels ("user is over-reliant"). Require ≥2 consistent observations across different tasks before proposing a persistent change ([Recommendation]; threshold is a design choice to be tested, not evidence-derived).
  2. **Inspectable by default, negotiable on demand** (OLM evidence: inspectable preferred; negotiation improves accuracy). Editing is available but not required.
  3. **Risk-based persistence** (B5): session-level adaptation automatic; cross-session profile changes need confirmation.
  4. **Adapt on stakes and task, not only on person**: the same user gets more verification scaffolding on high-stakes/out-of-frontier tasks.
  5. **Fade by default, restore on evidence**: reduce guidance when the user produces good frames unprompted; restore on errors (expertise reversal + fading).
  6. **Don't optimize the loop on self-report or speed**; use verified outcomes and voluntary reuse; treat satisfaction as a guardrail metric.
  7. **Separate task performance from capability**: claim capability development only from later, comparable, less-aided tasks (Bastani, Shen & Tamkin show with-AI performance misleads).
- [Inference on T2] What can be shown *without* persistent memory: within-session framing → better outcome (dimensions 1–2) and within-session collaboration behaviors (dimension 3, partially). What *requires* multi-session data: personalization accuracy, fading, and capability development (dimension 4). The "adaptive" claim is therefore the least testable part of the thesis in a K1 MVP.

### Gaps
- No study found that closes the Evaluation → Adaptation loop for *professional human-AI collaboration* and shows improved outcomes over time. This is the central empirical gap for the NB thesis.
- No evidence found on how users react to an AI showing them a model of their *collaboration weaknesses* (as opposed to knowledge mastery in OLMs) — possible defensiveness/autonomy concerns are untested.
- Delayed-retention and transfer data for AI-assisted professional work are essentially absent (Kestin 2025 has no delayed test; Shen & Tamkin measured immediately).
- How native platform memory (Claude/ChatGPT memory) handles user-editable inferred traits was not covered here (WS5).

## E. Cross-cutting limitations, risks, and counter-thesis notes relevant to WS4

### Takeaway
The evidence supports the *problem* NB targets (silent misframing, over-reliance, miscalibrated self-assessment, the jagged frontier), but not yet NB's *solution* (adaptive coaching + 4-D evaluation + learning loop) in professional settings; the strongest direct evidence for any solution is narrow (discovery-as-separate-step on benchmarks; structured AI tutors in education).

### Cited Findings
- [Finding] Problem evidence: experts slowed down yet felt faster (METR 2025); AI users 19 pp worse outside frontier (BCG 2023); discernment drops with polished artifacts (Anthropic 2026); confidence in AI ↔ less critical thinking (Lee CHI 2025). — [METR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089); [Dell'Acqua et al.](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf); [Anthropic Fluency Index](https://anthropic.com/research/AI-fluency-index); [Lee et al.](https://advait.org/files/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf)
- [Finding] Solution evidence (narrow): discovery decoupled from execution closes most of the underspecification gap on SWE tasks (Edwards 2026); guarded AI tutor avoids learning harm (Bastani 2025); structured AI tutor beats active learning in one course (Kestin 2025). — [Edwards et al.](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:4fff7875-1a05-4c44-93e3-d570647cd685/?utm_source=claude_desktop); [Bastani et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html); [Kestin et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/)
- [Finding] Friction/preference evidence against heavy coaching: least-liked designs are most effective (Buçinca 2021); ~47% of student conversations are direct/minimal-effort (Anthropic 2025). — [Buçinca](https://consensus.app/papers/details/w:73713e06-5efc-4c9b-b0da-7408bdb871aa/?utm_source=claude_desktop); [Anthropic Education Report](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-education-report-how-university-students-use-claude)
- [Fact] Platforms are already shipping learning modes and fluency research (Study Mode, Learning Mode, Guided Learning; AI Fluency Index; reported fluency scorecard) — absorption risk for the coaching layer. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/artificial-intelligence/openai-launches-study-mode-in-chatgpt-to-promote-active-learning-check-ai-dependency); [Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/research/AI-fluency-index); [TestingCatalog (unverified)](https://testingcatalog.com/anthropic-to-introduce-personal-ai-fluency-scorecard-in-claude)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Steelman (b) "users want results, not better collaboration skills": supported by usage logs and friction studies. NB's response should be to sell *better outcomes* (framing that prevents silent misframing; verification that catches errors) and let capability development be a side effect — consistent with the brief's "skills grow naturally, not through constant lecturing."
- [Inference] Steelman (d) "evaluation adds complexity without value": partially supported — LLM judges are biased and self-report is unreliable, so a naive evaluation layer can produce confident but invalid signals. Counter: evaluation anchored in the user's own acceptance criteria and executable checks is cheap and valid; heavy collaboration/capability scoring should be deferred.
- [Inference] Steelman (a) "platforms absorb the value": strongest for coaching/learning modes and fluency measurement (vendors have the logs); weakest for cross-platform problem framing tied to a user's own acceptance criteria — though "Ask or Assume?"-style clarification could also be absorbed into agents as models improve.
- [Inference] Equity risk: reflection/forcing interventions benefit high Need-for-Cognition users more (Buçinca 2021; Li CHI 2026), so an adaptive coach may help those who need it least unless coaching is lightweight and stakes-triggered.
- [Recommendation] For the first experiment (K1), evaluate only: (1) outcome quality against user-confirmed acceptance criteria vs. a plain-chat baseline (blind pairwise, cross-family judge + human check), (2) framing quality via a pre-execution frame checklist and "misframing rework" count, (3) a few behavioral collaboration signals (verification actions, corrections), and (4) voluntary reuse. Do not claim capability development or adaptive personalization from a ≤4-week, ≤10-user study.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head evidence comparing a "coaching layer" against native learning modes or plain use for professional tasks.
- No evidence on the cost-effectiveness (time cost of discovery/coaching vs. outcome gain) for knowledge workers; this must be measured in NB's own experiment.
- Several 2026 sources above are preprints (arXiv/medRxiv) and not yet peer reviewed; findings may shift.
